## Bad data
- Insufficient quantity of training data.
	- Very different ML algorithms have comparable performance, if given enough training data.
- Nonrepresentative training data.
	- It is crucial to use a training set that is representative of the cases you want to generalize to.
	- If the sample is too small you will have [[6. Sampling noise|sampling noise]].
	- Also large samples can be flawed because of [[7. Sampling bias|sampling bias]].
- Poor quality data.
	- Data full of errors, outliers and [[2. Noise|noise]].
	- Obvious outliers could be omitted or fixed.
	- In case of instances missing certain features:
		- The features could be ignored completely.
		- The instances could be ignored.
		- The missing features could be filled, e.g. interpolated.
		- Train two models; one with and one without the features and compare them.
- Irrelevant features.
	- The training data should contain enough relevant features and not too many irrelevant ones.
	- Requires proper [[1. Feature engineering|feature engineering]].
